# The Horse is Technology (Thich Nhat Hanh, 10 Nov 2013)

Dharma talk by Thich Nhat Hanh, 10 November 2013, Stillwater Meditation Hall, Upper Hamlet, Plum Village (94 min). He gave it five days before the 90-day winter retreat, and two and a half weeks after his day at Google (23 October 2013).

## Outline

Timestamps are for the MP3. Subtract about 11:52 to find the same point in the YouTube video. The outline is a paraphrase, not a transcript.

- **12:26 – Winter retreat.** Studying Buddhist psychology (*Vijñaptimātratā*, "manifestation only") this winter, and the coming ordination and 21-day retreat.
- **17:56 – Rules for the retreat.** No personal email, no internet, no Facebook, and only Dharma content on devices. Facebook is "neither good nor evil", but the way we use it brings more harm than good and takes us away from ourselves.
- **23:22 – The day at Google.** Breakfast, walking meditation across three sites, the talk, mindful lunch, deep relaxation, then a long afternoon session with executives and engineers.
- **29:06 – Google's questions.** "Intention, Insight, and Innovation". How can technology integrate rather than destroy? Emotional health at work is falling and distress rising, and staff burn out and can't detach from work. Thay notes the wish for a technological fix to technology addiction and asks whether we can heal addiction with the thing we are addicted to.
- **38:50 – The horse.** A rider galloping past is asked where he is going and says to ask the horse. The horse is technology. It carries us and we have lost control. The place to begin is intention: what do we really want?
- **40:52 – The cost.** Chasing wealth and being number one costs us our lives, our families and nature. Most of us consume to cover up the suffering inside, and technology helps us run away from ourselves. Yet technology that helps us go home to ourselves "goes in a good way".
- **47:29 – The four nutriments.** Sense impressions: we go online to feel something and so avoid ourselves (the example is a teenager addicted to games). If monastics do the same they cannot help the world, which is why the retreat rules are a practice and not a loss.
- **58:52 – Volition.** What do you want to do with your life? Money, power and fame are not enough. A leader with bodhicitta can reverse the direction of civilisation and "control the horse". Technology is like a knife, which can cook or kill.
- **1:05:44 – Consciousness as food.** We water good or bad seeds, alone and as a collective.
- **1:13:44 – Technology that sends us home.** A mindfulness website, phones that ring a mindfulness bell, and devices that tell you when you are too angry to act. A young engineer worried this would impose on people. Thay answered that there are real needs and unreal ones, and good technology helps people tell them apart.
- **1:21:35 – Work and life are not two.** Walking from the car park, washing dishes, calligraphy with tea in the ink. The four parts of monastic life (study, practice, work, play) each contain the others.
